SubZona

Typical real-estate apps work if you're staying in one place. SubZona is for the semester you're not, matching students headed abroad with students who need a place while they're gone.

PlatformiOS · Android
FocusMarketplace & trust design
ContextUniversity of Arizona
The Problem

The lease calendar doesn't match the school calendar

Off-campus housing is built around 12-month leases, priced and marketed for renters who plan to stay a year. But a large slice of students only need housing for a single semester, and none of the major platforms are built to handle that.

"I need a place for four months. Every listing site is built around a full year lease."

Going abroad: students studying abroad for a semester are stuck paying rent on an empty room, breaking their lease outright, or handing their apartment to a stranger off Craigslist with no way to verify who they are.

Coming to campus: exchange students, transfers, and interns search those same year-lease platforms for something that doesn't exist there, or fall back on unverified Facebook posts with no real structure.

Who It's For

Two students, opposite problem, same semester

SubZona works because it's anchored to one matched pair: someone leaving, and someone arriving. Every feature exists to optimize this matching system that naturally exists on college campuses.

Outbound

Jordan

Junior · Business · Studying in Barcelona, Spring

Signed a 12-month lease before deciding to go abroad. Doesn't want to break it or pay rent on an empty room, and doesn't want to hand his apartment off to a stranger.

"I just need someone to take over my place for the Spring semester."
Inbound

Maya

Exchange student · Arriving Spring, one semester only

Never been to Tucson. Every apartment site wants a year-long signature; every Facebook group is unverified strangers with no real dates attached.

"I don't want a full lease. I just need something for the Spring that's close to campus."
How It Works

Built around the term, not the date

Every listing and search is built around Spring, Summer, or Fall, the unit students actually live in. Once two people are ready to talk, the platform steps back.

01

No verified university email, no listing, no browsing.

02

Home screen asks one question: find a place, or list one.

03

Term-first search

04

Request, then connect

Sending a request shares verified contact info directly. SubZona doesn't host the conversation on the platform.

Establishing the Direction

The action plan

A two-sided marketplace lives or dies on its first few hundred matches. V1 is scoped to get a listing posted and a request accepted, nothing more.

Decisions & Trade-offs

The features

Each of these elements where deliberately excluded/included to decrease liability and ensure smooth interactions within the app.

—

No in-app payments

Handling rent money makes SubZona a financial intermediary, with the liability and compliance burden that comes with it. Students settle payment directly, the same way they would with any sublease. It's slower to monetize, but it keeps SubZona out of a business it isn't built to run.

—

No in-app messaging

Hosting an ongoing chat means SubZona is responsible for whatever gets said or agreed to in it. Instead, an accepted request exchanges verified contact info and the conversation moves off-platform. 

—

.edu required to join

Ensures people using the app are students. This is meant to establish more trust on the platform, and establish a clear intended market of users. Users are more likely to understand the expectations of student living, the realities of shared housing, and the importance of finding a reliable roommate

—

Term-first, not date-first search

Modeling Spring/Summer/Fall instead of a generic date range makes it easier for the matching system to identify students with overlapping availability.

List your place

Dates first, details second

Roadmap

From one campus to a real marketplace


Phase 1

Prove the loop

Launch at U of A for one semester cycle. Get to a real fill rate before adding anything else.

Phase 2

Deepen trust

ID verification badges, post-sublease ratings, and stronger reporting make the match itself more trustworthy, not the transaction.

Phase 3

Scale the calendar

Expand to campuses with strong study-abroad populations, since the same semester mismatch exists everywhere.

A two-sided marketplace doesn't always need more features. It needs one constraint everyone already agrees on.

The academic semester is that constraint. Anchoring search, listings, and trust to it made almost every other decision easier.
